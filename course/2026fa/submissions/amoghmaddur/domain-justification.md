# Domain Justification — creative-technical SOC arbitrage

## Executive summary

Why this recipe exists for one kind of student, and two ways its numbers could mislead.

## Who, and when

An international master's student on **F-1 OPT, year 1, STEM-eligible**, who builds
programmatic video and explainer tooling: scene-graph animation, render pipelines,
TTS-timed composition. The same work is honestly "Motion Designer", "Product
Designer", "Creative Technologist" or "Front-End Engineer". They run the mode **once,
in the first two weeks**, before writing the résumé headline.

## The information asymmetry

A job board shows openings, not **sponsorship history by title**. In the engine's
30,369-company file, 2 employers list a sponsored motion-design title, 18 a front-end
title, and 55 a product/interface-design title; none list "Creative Technologist".
Without this, rejection for motion roles reads as personal failure, when the variable
was the noun on the posting. The mode also prices the advice to "just call yourself a
product designer": each title's occupation, its median wage ($61,300 graphic design vs
$133,080 software), and whether it has an automation-resilience score at all.

## Engine layers

**80 Days** (primary): a title-first read of the H-1B + Form D company file.
**Cognitive Pivot** (cost): wage and `cognitive_pivot_score` per framing. It surfaced
that this student's pivot targets, 15-1255 and 15-1299, are unscored. **Job-Ops**
(downstream): liveness checks start only after a title is chosen, and the mode says so.

## Failure modes

**1. A broad title and a summarised list inflate the gap, in the same direction.** My
first run reported a **150× spread**: 450 employers for "Front-End / Software
Engineer", 3 for motion. Reading the rows, 441 of the 450 came from the generic probe
`SOFTWARE ENGINEER`, which catches QA, infrastructure and manager titles this
portfolio cannot claim. The title field also lists only "common roles" (97.4% of
companies have more approvals than titles listed), so rare creative titles are the
ones dropped. Both push toward *"call yourself an engineer"*; the honest claimable
spread was 55 vs 2, led by product design. **Hardest to catch for** a student and
advisor shown one decisive number. It breaks only when someone reads the rows.

**2. The title→SOC mapping is mine, and errs in the good-news direction.** The file
has no SOC column. If "Product Designer" is mapped to 15-1255 ($98,090) but an
employer files it nearer 15-1252 ($133,080), the mode understates the wage of the very
title the student picks, and nothing contradicts it until a low offer. **Hardest to
catch for** someone with no occupational home. A biostatistician knows they are
15-2041; a render-pipeline builder does not, which is why they cannot audit this
assumption.
